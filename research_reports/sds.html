01 / INVESTMENT SUMMARY

The recovery case is about
operating leverage, not hype.

SDS does not need a spectacular bakery boom to work. At RM0.385, the market is valuing the company at roughly 7.8x FY26 earnings. The opportunity is that FY26 earnings were depressed by weaker retail demand and cost absorption, while the underlying manufacturing, distribution and cash-generating platform remains intact.

Core thesis. Malaysia's consumer economy is already recovering. The missing piece is SDS-specific conversion: retail revenue needs to catch up with the broader market, while Mamee and better route productivity allow revenue growth to flow through the fixed-cost base.

Why we are positive

  • Valuation is low relative to normalized earnings potential.
  • FY26 operating cash flow of ~RM48.7m was well above PAT of ~RM26.8m.
  • Wholesale provides a more resilient base; retail provides the higher operating leverage.
  • Existing factories and distribution infrastructure are already substantial.
  • Mamee adds longer-shelf-life products that can use the existing network.

Why this is not a high-quality compounder

  • FY26 PAT fell ~19% and retail segment profit fell ~29%.
  • Moat is operational, not structural or wide.
  • Oil/diesel costs remain a margin risk for a ~340-vehicle distribution fleet.
  • RM101.7m Tebrau land is large relative to SDS's market value.
  • Future factory capex and funding requirements are still uncertain.
02 / FY26 EARNINGS RESET

FY26 was the reset.
The damage was concentrated in retail.

03 / CONSUMER & MACRO

We are not waiting for Malaysians
to start spending again.

The broader Malaysian consumer picture is better than the SDS FY26 numbers might imply. Private consumption, retail volume and business confidence are positive. The issue is that spending remains selective and the accumulated price level is still high. For SDS, the key variable is whether the macro recovery reaches its retail stores.

Why consumers can still feel squeezed

  • Lower inflation means prices are rising more slowly, not that prices have returned to old levels.
  • Food, housing, transport and services continue to consume household budgets.
  • Formal-sector median monthly wage was ~RM3,027 in March 2026, but income distribution remains wide.
  • Elevated oil/diesel prices remain relevant to both households and SDS's logistics costs.

Why 2027 could improve the setup

  • Employment and wage growth remain supportive.
  • Retail trade volume is expanding rather than contracting.
  • Wholesale/retail confidence improved materially in Q3 2026.
  • A higher minimum-wage floor, if implemented, could support lower-income purchasing power.
The test we care about. If Malaysia retail grows around 4% while SDS retail remains flat, the problem is company-specific. If SDS retail reaches 5–8%, the operating leverage thesis becomes much more credible. At 8%+ retail growth with ~35% gross margin, the probability of a re-rating rises materially.
Oil is a double-edged macro variable. Elevated crude and diesel can suppress household purchasing power and increase SDS distribution costs. Conversely, normalization toward a lower oil-price environment could provide a double tailwind through consumer affordability and logistics margins.
04 / OPERATING PLATFORM

The infrastructure is already built.
Now it has to earn more.

SDS has approximately 25 distribution centres and a fleet of roughly 340 vehicles, alongside its existing manufacturing plants and retail network. This is both an advantage and a risk: the infrastructure supports scale, but under-utilization creates operating leverage in the wrong direction.

Manufacturing capacity. Kempas and Seremban together have been described at roughly 12,000 buns/hour, 3,000 loaves/hour and around 600 cakes/day. Historical utilization has been around 70–80%, with recent research pointing to roughly 80%. That implies some existing headroom, although exact product-mix constraints are not fully disclosed.

Retail strategy

  • Network was around 42 outlets in the latest detailed disclosure, including bakery, café and bakery-café formats.
  • FY25 added six outlets, but FY27 plans are more measured at roughly three new outlets plus renovations.
  • The strategy is shifting toward productivity, rebranding and menu refresh rather than aggressive footprint growth.
  • Same-store sales and profit per outlet are therefore more important than outlet count.

Distribution strategy

  • Fleet expansion raised costs before revenue fully caught up.
  • Q1 FY26 selling/distribution expense increased materially, reflecting fleet maintenance and consolidation costs.
  • The opportunity is to improve route density and asset utilization.
  • Mamee's longer-shelf-life products can potentially increase utilization of the same network.
05 / MAMEE ACQUISITION

RM28m bought SDS
a new earnings option.

SDS acquired Mamee Bakery assets for RM28m, including factory lots, production lines, machinery/equipment and trademarks/know-how. The product set includes London/Tora Swiss Rolls, layer cakes and bear cakes. These products matter because longer shelf life can widen distribution and reduce expiry pressure compared with fresh bakery products.

What is known

  • Plant 1 is operational.
  • Plant 2 is under refurbishment and Plant 3 is to be upgraded sequentially.
  • Commercial production is targeted around FY27, with analyst expectations for progressive contribution.
  • Longer shelf life should make wider Peninsular distribution and Singapore/export more practical.

What is not known

  • Exact capacity and utilization.
  • Historical revenue and gross margin of the acquired assets.
  • Incremental employee and operating costs.
  • Full capex required to upgrade all plants.
  • Precise revenue and PAT contribution. We therefore do not assign aggressive valuation credit today.
Our hurdle. We want to see at least RM4–5m incremental PAT over time before treating the acquisition as clearly value-accretive. The strategic fit is good; the financial return still needs to be demonstrated.
06 / TEBRAU CAPITAL ALLOCATION

The biggest risk is not
the bakery. It is what comes next.

SDS agreed to acquire approximately 35.37 acres in Tebrau, Johor for RM101.7m. The purchase price is broadly in line with the stated land valuation, so the issue is not an obvious overpayment. The issue is the capital commitment that follows.

What happens next

  • Transaction became unconditional in Aug 2026.
  • Current zoning is residential; industrial conversion and any premium remain relevant.
  • Management must determine layout, construction requirements and production needs.
  • Future development cost and phasing are not yet sufficiently disclosed.

Why we stay conservative

  • RM101.7m is roughly 2.1x FY26 operating cash flow.
  • It is more than three times current cash.
  • Debt or other financing will likely become important.
  • We assign no meaningful earnings value to a future factory until capex, capacity, utilization and ROIC are visible.
Capital-allocation thesis breaker: if SDS funds a large Tebrau factory before existing capacity and the Mamee assets are well utilized, the balance-sheet risk could overwhelm the recovery thesis. Tebrau should be phased and return-driven, not treated as automatic growth.

11 / RISKS & THESIS BREAKERS

The downside is straightforward:
the recovery may not arrive at SDS.

Primary risks

  • Consumer mismatch: Malaysia's retail market improves while SDS retail stays weak.
  • Input/logistics inflation: oil, diesel, packaging and labour costs offset revenue recovery.
  • Mamee execution: acquired capacity ramps slowly or earns poor returns.
  • Competition: Gardenia, Massimo and other bakery players constrain pricing and shelf space.
  • Capital allocation: Tebrau creates financing and capex pressure before returns are visible.
  • Small-cap liquidity: valuation can remain discounted even when fundamentals improve.

Hard thesis killers

1. SDS retail remains negative for several quarters despite broad retail growth.
2. Mamee requires additional capital but cannot demonstrate at least an acceptable return.
3. Tebrau becomes a large debt-funded construction programme before current assets are efficiently utilized.
4. Operating cash flow deteriorates materially below accounting earnings.

13 / FINAL VERDICT

BUY / ACCUMULATE.
Let the numbers prove it.

At RM0.385, SDS is a legitimate small-cap value/recovery opportunity rather than a no-brainer compounder. We are effectively paying around 8x depressed FY26 earnings for a business with healthy cash conversion, meaningful physical infrastructure and several plausible FY27 catalysts.
